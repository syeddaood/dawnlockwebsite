Every alarm app promises to get you up. Almost none of them survive contact
with 6am-you — the half-asleep version of yourself who swipes, taps snooze,
or just mutes the phone without ever really waking up.

## The problem isn't the sound

Louder alarms, vibration patterns, escalating volume — none of it matters if
the action that stops the ringing takes less conscious effort than staying
asleep. A swipe gesture or a tap on "dismiss" is something your thumb can do
on autopilot, no brain required.

## What actually works

The only reliable fix is forcing a small amount of real cognitive or physical
effort before the alarm will stop:

- **Solve something.** A math problem, a memory sequence, a typed phrase —
  anything that needs working memory, not muscle memory.
- **Move something.** A shake gesture or a few reps forces blood flowing and
  a body that's already out of bed.
- **Prove something.** A photo of a real object, or holding the phone level
  and still, can't be done half-asleep by accident.

That's the whole idea behind Cukkoo's wake-up missions — the alarm doesn't
stop until one of these is actually completed, for real, not performed on
autopilot.

## No escape hatches

The other half of the problem is timing: most people don't decide to snooze
in the moment, they decide it the night before, by leaving the alarm
editable. Locking the alarm a few hours before it rings removes that
decision entirely — by the time it's ringing, there's nothing left to
negotiate with.
