"Eight hours a night" gets repeated so often it's treated as a universal
law, but sleep need actually shifts quite a bit across a lifetime. Sleeping
to the wrong number for your age can leave you tired even when you're
technically "getting enough."

## General guidelines by age

These are the ranges most sleep health organizations broadly agree on for
healthy adults and children:

- **Newborns (0–3 months):** 14–17 hours
- **Infants (4–11 months):** 12–15 hours
- **Toddlers (1–2 years):** 11–14 hours
- **Preschoolers (3–5 years):** 10–13 hours
- **School-age children (6–12 years):** 9–12 hours
- **Teenagers (13–18 years):** 8–10 hours
- **Adults (18–64 years):** 7–9 hours
- **Older adults (65+):** 7–8 hours

These are ranges, not targets to hit exactly — the right number for you
depends on genetics, activity level, and overall health, not just age.

## Why teenagers are a special case

Teenagers are often blamed for "staying up too late," but adolescence comes
with a genuine biological shift: the release of melatonin shifts later in
the evening, pushing the natural sleep window later too. Combined with
early school start times, this is a large part of why teens are
chronically under-slept — it's a mismatch between biology and schedule, not
just a discipline problem.

## Why "I function fine on 5 hours" is usually wrong

A small fraction of the population genuinely carries a rare genetic
variant that allows them to function well on less sleep. Almost everyone
who believes they're in that group is not — they've simply adapted to
chronic sleep debt, which dulls the *feeling* of tiredness without removing
its effects on memory, mood, and reaction time.

## The practical takeaway

Rather than fixating on hitting an exact number, two signals matter more
day to day:

- **Do you wake up without an alarm most days,** close to your usual time?
- **Do you stay reasonably alert through the afternoon** without needing
  a nap or heavy caffeine to function?

If the answer to both is consistently no, the fix is almost always more
sleep, not a better morning routine — no wake-up trick, Cukkoo included,
makes up for a real sleep deficit. What a strong wake-up routine *can* do is
make sure the sleep you did get actually translates into getting up when
you intended to, instead of evaporating into a dozen snoozed minutes.
