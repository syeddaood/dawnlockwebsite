Two nights of exactly 7.5 hours of sleep can feel completely different the
next morning. The difference usually isn't the sleep itself — it's the exact
moment within a sleep cycle that you got pulled out of it.

## The four-stage cycle

A typical sleep cycle lasts around 90 minutes and moves through several
stages:

- **Light sleep (N1/N2)** — the transition in and out of sleep. Easy to
  wake from, barely disorienting.
- **Deep sleep (N3)** — slow-wave sleep, where the body repairs tissue and
  consolidates physical recovery. Waking from this stage is where grogginess
  hits hardest.
- **REM sleep** — the dreaming stage, tied to memory processing and
  emotional regulation. Waking here can leave a lingering, dream-like
  disorientation.

Across a full night, you cycle through these stages four to six times, with
deep sleep concentrated earlier in the night and REM periods lengthening
toward morning.

## Why the exact wake-up moment matters

Getting pulled out of deep sleep is what produces the heavy, almost
hungover feeling known as sleep inertia. Getting woken during light sleep,
by contrast, tends to feel almost effortless — you're already close to the
surface.

The problem is that a fixed alarm time has no idea which stage you're in. A
time that works perfectly on Monday can land you mid-deep-sleep on Tuesday,
simply because you fell asleep at a slightly different time or your night
was slightly more fragmented.

## What you can actually do with this

You can't precisely control which stage you're in at a given minute without
specialized tracking, but you can shift the odds in your favor:

- **Sleep in 90-minute multiples.** Working backward from your wake time in
  ~90-minute blocks (e.g. 6, 7.5, or 9 hours) increases the chance you
  surface near a natural light-sleep transition rather than mid-cycle.
- **Keep a consistent bedtime.** Irregular sleep times make your cycle
  timing unpredictable, which makes a fixed alarm more likely to catch you
  at a bad moment.
- **Accept that some grogginess is inevitable.** Even with good timing,
  you'll occasionally get caught in deep sleep. What matters more than
  avoiding it entirely is not letting that grogginess talk you into going
  back under — which is exactly the moment a snooze button is most
  dangerous, and exactly why Cukkoo doesn't give you one.
